A . P . J Abdul Kalam

1931 - 2015

Missile Man of India

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (1931–2015) was an Indian aerospace scientist, educator, author, and the 11th President of India (2002–2007). He is popularly known as the “Missile Man of India” for his major contribution to India’s missile and space programmes. Born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam came from a modest family. He studied physics and later aerospace engineering. He worked with DRDO and ISRO, where he played an important role in India’s space and defence technology programmes. He was closely associated with the SLV-III project, which successfully placed the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980, and later contributed to the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme. Kalam also played a significant role in India’s Pokhran-II nuclear tests in 1998, working alongside scientists and government leaders. His scientific career and leadership in strategic technology projects earned him national recognition. In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India. Unlike the traditional image of a distant political figure, he became especially popular among students and young people. He frequently visited schools and universities and encouraged young Indians to pursue education, scientific thinking, innovation, and ambitious goals. He wrote several influential books, including Wings of Fire, his autobiography, and Ignited Minds, which focuses on India's youth and the country's potential. Kalam received India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna, in 1997, along with numerous other awards and honorary degrees.
